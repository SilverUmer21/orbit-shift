# Trailer B — "Special Delivery"

Owner brief (2026-10-19): a better, creative, refined motion-graphics gameplay trailer built from the newer graphics, with proper text and gameplay. Screens must switch slowly enough that people can see and understand them. **Owner's answers:** promo trailer video, **no longer than 45 s** with the important details shown slowly as an overview, opening on the Special delivery envelope, and featuring Results with the reward card, the Hangar Sorting Room and the Mailbag / Night Post.

## 1. What was wrong with Trailer A ("The Night Mail", 30 s)

- **Too many beats.** It packs 8 scenes and 3 sub-beats into 30 s. Thread, Feel the heat and Dodge get 2 s each, and a cream page-turn covers about 0.5 s of each one.
- **The rules are never shown.** Captions name things ("One tap", "Hold"), but nothing shows that a tap reverses, that you must be at the gap, or that grazing the edge scores a PERFECT.
- **Captions are hard to read.** They are white text with drop shadows over busy gameplay, they sit next to the HUD counters, and some stay on screen for under 1 s.
- **None of the newer art appears.** The cut has no first-launch Home with the title, no LEVEL CLEAR results with paper stars, no reward card, no in-run PERFECT tags (disabled), no Sorting Room, no postcards. The end card lasts 2 s.

## 2. Research (store video and trailer practice)

| Finding | Source | How it shapes Trailer B |
|---|---|---|
| Google Play autoplays the first ~30 s muted; Google asks for ≥80% real app footage | AppFollow, AppRadar, Gummicube | The whole core loop (tap, gap, perfect, hold) is taught by 0:26. Everything except the envelope and end card is the real game. |

| Subtitle reading rule of thumb: ~3 words/s, at least ~1.5–2 s per line | Subtitle practice (Netflix, BBC timing guides) | Every caption stays about 2 s or longer; most stay 3–6 s. |
| Explainer trailers (e.g. Mini Metro, Alto) let the world carry the frame and keep cuts calm | Team Alto interviews, genre practice | One long take for the core loop; the camera moves slowly; transitions are paper folds that belong to the Night Post world. |

## 3. Concept

**The trailer is a letter Pip delivers to the viewer.** It opens on an envelope addressed "To: You". Pip taps the wax seal, it cracks, and the letter unfolds into the live game. The scene changes after that are folds of the same letter. Everything is told in the Night Post's own language: paper tags, creases, wax and gold postmarks.

## 5. Design rules

- **Captions** are die-cut Night Post tags:
  - Cream paper with a hard-split fold shade, a cut lip and a punched string hole. Text is Night 900 in Lilita One (≈100 px at 1080 wide), and the key word sits on a gold marker band (gold = action, as in the UI).
  - In: unfold from a crease (0.35 s, slight overshoot). A change of line flips the tag over its crease (0.3 s). Out: fold shut (0.25 s).
  - Every line stays about 2 s or longer; most stay 3–6 s.
  - The tags live in one zone per screen type: under the HUD in runs, and over the least important strip on menu screens.
- **Motion-graphic annotations** sit in game coordinates (they move with the camera) and appear only while their idea is on screen:
  - A gold dashed arrow ahead of the glider that retracts and regrows on each tap.
  - A gold landing zone on the orbit under a gate's gap, with dashed guides from the gap edges.
  - A glint on the edge the glider will graze.
  - A paper fingertip below the orbit with a dashed tap ripple, or a filling ring for a hold.
- **Transitions** are freeze-frame paper folds of the outgoing shot, 0.6–0.8 s each: a letter fold into thirds that slides away, or a crease flip. A transition never covers a moment the scene is showing.
- **Camera:** slow eased push-ins only, with the zoom never above 1.7×. Slow motion is used once, for the PERFECT.
- **Footage:** the real game on the virtual clock. Staged gates are real gates whose gap is shifted so the game's own rules score the result. The in-run PERFECT tags stay on.
- **Sound:** synthesised from the render's event log, as in Trailer A. A music-box lullaby plays under the story, paper percussion under the take, bells on gates, a thud and a gold chord on the postmark, and the final chord on the logo.
